\section*{UDL and Inclusion Supports}
\subsection*{Multiple Representation}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Introduce AI with a video, teacher talk, and concrete examples from daily life.
\item Use visible category labels and color coding when sorting examples.
\item Keep the difference between “example” and “guess” explicit.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Multiple Action / Expression}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Allow students to answer by sorting, pointing, talking, or dragging items.
\item Pair students so one can navigate while the other explains.
\item Provide printed category cards for off-screen rehearsal before the digital task.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Multiple Engagement}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Connect ocean cleanup to a meaningful real-world problem.
\item Alternate short discussion bursts with active sorting.
\item Treat mistakes as useful training data, not failure.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How to Improve Inclusion Next Time}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Add picture-supported category cards for students who need more off-screen scaffolding.
\item Include a bias/error conversation using one intentionally tricky example.
\item Prepare sentence stems such as “I put it here because…” and “The computer guessed wrong because…”
\item Save screenshots of correct and incorrect classifications in \texttt{artifacts/} as evidence examples.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Teacher Moves / Script / Routines}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Model login and launch before discussing AI concepts.
\item Stop after the first classification to ask what the computer is learning.
\item Normalize wrong guesses as part of training the model.
\item Close with a short reflection on where students see sorting or prediction in real life.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Common Errors and Debugging}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Students may confuse AI with a human-like robot instead of a trained model.
\item Login friction can consume too much time if not tightly modeled.
\item Students may sort by appearance without explaining their category logic.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Extensions and Simplifications}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Extension: compare two hard-to-sort examples and debate the best category.
\item Simplification: reduce the task to fewer categories and more teacher-led sorting.
\item Extension: connect the lesson to responsible AI and why bad data causes bad results.
\end{itemize}
