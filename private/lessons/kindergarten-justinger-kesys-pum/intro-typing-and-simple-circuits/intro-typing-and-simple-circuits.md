## ♿ UDL and Inclusion Supports

### 👀 Multiple Representation
- 👀 Show keys visually and physically point to them on the keyboard.
- 👀 Model circuit parts one at a time before handing out all materials.
- 👀 Use color and spatial language consistently: here, there, together, connected.

### ✍️ Multiple Action / Expression
- ✍️ Allow partner-supported keyboarding and hand-over-hand support when needed.
- ✍️ Offer pre-portioned materials and physically larger examples.
- ✍️ Accept explanation through pointing, showing, or brief oral response.

### ❤️ Multiple Engagement
- ❤️ Use the science activity as a motivating payoff for the typing warm-up.
- ❤️ Keep the typing segment short and success-based.
- ❤️ Make the transition clear so students know when one task ends and the next begins.

## 🌱 How We Are Already Making It Inclusive

## 🔧 How to Improve Inclusion Next Time

- 🔧 Add visual step mats for both the keyboard task and the circuit build.
- 🔧 Prepare one complete exemplar circuit at each table for students who need a concrete anchor.
- 🔧 Add sentence stems such as “It worked because…” and “We need to fix…”
- 🔧 Create a quieter typing option or reduced-key version for students who fatigue quickly.

## 🗣️ Teacher Moves / Script / Routines

- 🗣️ Model the typing task with only the needed keys visible in student attention.
- 🗣️ Pause before the circuit release to restate one safety rule and one build rule.
- 🗣️ Circulate first to tables that need connection help rather than re-explaining to the whole group.
- 🗣️ Close by asking students to show the part that made the system work.

## 🐞 Common Errors and Debugging

- 🐞 Students may confuse keyboard keys or lose left/right hand positioning.
- 🐞 Students may mix Play-Doh colors before building and lose structural clarity.
- 🐞 Circuit failures will often come from incomplete contact or reversed/loose placement rather than misunderstanding the concept.

## ↔️ Extensions and Simplifications

- ↔️ Extension: have students compare two circuit builds and say which connection is stronger.
- ↔️ Simplification: use a teacher demo circuit if student independence is too high a load.
- ↔️ Extension: connect typing “input” to the idea that circuits also need the right path to work.
