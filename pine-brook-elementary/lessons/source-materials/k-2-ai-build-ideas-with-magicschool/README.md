# K-2 AI Build Ideas with MagicSchool

## Purpose

Students use the classroom MagicSchool link in Seesaw to ask AI for build ideas based on the material at their table. Students choose one idea, build with their group, and share how AI helped.

## Materials

- Chromebook or classroom device
- Seesaw link to the prepared MagicSchool room
- Table materials such as Zoobs, LEGO, Strawbees, KEVA Planks, or another building material
- Optional: photo capture or Seesaw post for student share-out

## Slide Deck Files

- `k-2-ai-build-ideas-with-magicschool.pptx`: editable slide deck that can be uploaded to Google Slides
- `k-2-ai-build-ideas-with-magicschool.pdf`: PDF preview/share copy

Upload `k-2-ai-build-ideas-with-magicschool.pptx` to Google Drive and open it with Google Slides. The deck is intentionally simple, with large text and copy-ready prompts for K-2 students.

## NYS CSDF Standards Alignment

The deck aligns to New York State Computer Science and Digital Fluency standards for early elementary learners:

- `K-1.IC.1`: Students compare doing a task with and without computing technology.
- `K-1.IC.2`: Students follow classroom rules for using computing technology and digital information.
- `K-1.IC.6`: Students identify ways people interact with computing devices.
- `K-1.CT.4`: Students break a build task into smaller steps.
- `K-1.CT.6`: Students follow a short algorithm: ask, choose, build, fix, share.
- `K-1.CT.10`: Students collaboratively create a plan for a task.
- `K-1.CY.1`: Students identify why names and private information stay private.
- `K-1.DL.2`: Students communicate and work with others using digital tools.
- `K-1.DL.3`: Students use provided keywords and sentence frames in a digital tool.
- `K-1.DL.4`: Students use a digital tool to support creation of an artifact.
- `K-1.DL.7`: Students identify responsible AI and digital citizenship actions.
- Grade 2 bridge: `2-3.IC.1`, `2-3.CY.1`, `2-3.DL.3`, and `2-3.NSD.3`.

Official source: New York State K-12 Computer Science and Digital Fluency Learning Standards.

## Inclusion Supports

- Large text, minimal words, and repeated sentence frames
- Visual cues for table materials
- Flexible response modes: typing, partner typing, oral prompting, pointing, building, photo, or partner share
- Collaborative roles: typer, reader, builder, sharer
- Short prompts that support early readers, multilingual learners, and students who need predictable language
- Teacher-facing slide that names inclusion moves aligned with NYSED culturally responsive-sustaining practice
