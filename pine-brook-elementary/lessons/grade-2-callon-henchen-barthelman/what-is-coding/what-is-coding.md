- **Special education considerations:** Simplify Language & Concepts: Explain coding as “giving clear instructions to make something happen.” Use simple, repeated phrases and visuals to show what an algorithm (set of instructions) is. Introduce directional words (up, down, left, right) with pictures and gestures. Use Multi-Sensory, Hands-On Activities: Use floor grids with masking tape and large arrow manipulatives to physically act out movements. Let students move a robot or themselves step-by-step on the grid to follow instructions. Use picture cards for steps in building a sandcastle to practice sequencing. Teach Explicitly, One Step at a Time: Model coding the path together as a whole class. Use a visual “coding tracker” to record steps. | Guide students to create a second code with a new goal (e.g., collecting sand) emphasizing differences. | Support Communication & Choice-Making: Use alternative communication methods (PECS, speech devices, gesture cards) to let students share ideas or choices. | Provide sentence starters or simple scripts for students to explain their code (“I go right, then up”). | Allow students to contribute by making choices with visuals rather than requiring verbal answers. | For some classes, teach the lesson as a whole group with students responding by pointing, pressing buttons, or choosing pictures. | Use assistive technology to allow engagement for nonverbal or physically limited students.
