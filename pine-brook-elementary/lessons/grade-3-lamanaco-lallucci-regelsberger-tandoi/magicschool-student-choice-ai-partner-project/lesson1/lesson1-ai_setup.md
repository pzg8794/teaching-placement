# Lesson 1 AI Setup

Use this setup for Session 1: Brainstorm and Plan.

## Goal

Students leave Lesson 1 with:
- one topic they care about
- a clear CS connection
- one product choice
- a high-level guide with 3 to 5 parts, sections, or questions

## Teacher Setup Steps

1. Open the teacher-managed MagicSchool or MagicStudent space for the class.
2. Make sure students enter using the school-approved access method, such as SSO or a teacher room code.
3. Limit students to the tools needed for Lesson 1 only:
   - `Idea Generator`
   - `AI Assistant`
4. Do not open full drafting or broad research tools for this lesson unless the class specifically needs them.
5. Project the approved prompt frames before students begin.
6. Review the AI safety rules:
   - no student names or private information
   - AI is a helper, not the final answer
   - students must check whether the AI response fits their goal
   - teacher can monitor activity
7. Give every student a planning sheet or digital planning document.
8. Tell students they must get teacher approval before moving to Lesson 2.

## Tool Roles

### Idea Generator

Use this tool first to:
- generate possible topics from student interests
- connect interests to computer science
- help students choose one or two possible directions

### AI Assistant

Use this tool after students have a possible topic to:
- check whether the topic is really connected to computer science
- narrow, improve, or refocus the topic
- generate a high-level guide with 3 to 5 parts, sections, or questions

## Lesson 1 Workflow

1. Student identifies an interest.
2. Student uses `Idea Generator` to generate CS-connected project options.
3. Student selects one or two possible topics.
4. Student uses the `AI Assistant` to:
   - test the CS connection
   - narrow the topic
   - generate a high-level project guide
5. Student records the final topic, product choice, and agreed-upon guide.
6. Teacher conferences and approves the plan.

## Teacher Look-Fors

- students stay inside the assigned tools
- students use AI for brainstorming and planning, not copying
- students can explain why the topic belongs in computer science
- students finish with a topic, product choice, and a usable high-level guide
