## The clear teaching offer to make

Offer your SBTE this specific support:

> Yesterday you asked for two agendas, a short opening while you take attendance, and another team-building activity before Friday's technology work. I found the Chapter 1 sequence in the book and prepared a short deck. For the marketing class, I can start with the last-purchase question, then have teams classify one industry or app as a good, a service, and an idea. I can put the steps on the board, keep time, circulate, and hand the lesson back to you. Would you like that today, or should I use your existing plan?

This is the teaching contribution: **content support plus a predictable routine**, not a separate CS lesson. The Chapter 1 ideas remain the business-course content. The CS connection is the way students work: identify the task, break it into steps, classify examples, explain a choice, check the result, and improve the artifact. The routine gives students a quiet entry, reduces working-memory load, uses a visible time boundary, and moves from individual thinking to partner/team talk before whole-class sharing. These are universal supports for attention and participation; they do not label students, diagnose ADHD, or create an accommodation without the SBTE/school process.

### What you are offering to do

1. Write or display the four-step agenda before students settle: **read the prompt → think/write → talk with a partner/team → share one idea**.
2. Give one direction at a time and point to the current step.
3. Provide a visible two- or three-minute timer and a one-minute warning.
4. Let students write, sketch, or quietly rehearse before speaking; do not require personal disclosure or cold-call a student.
5. Circulate with one neutral prompt: **“What is your group’s idea so far?”**
6. Collect only two or three different ideas, then hand the content connection to the SBTE’s existing lesson.

Choose one role for yourself—board agenda, timer, circulation, or debrief. The SBTE remains responsible for attendance, behavior decisions, grading, and the lesson transition.

## Two ready-to-use, low-stakes agenda cards

Use only after the SBTE chooses one. Keep both ungraded unless the SBTE explicitly changes that. The first card is the strongest match to the book and the additional planning transcript.

### Option A — Marketing: “How did marketing affect your last purchase?”

**Board-ready agenda**

1. Think of the last product you purchased; it may be a good, service, or idea.
2. Quietly write, sketch, or select one factor: **How did marketing affect your decision?**
3. With a partner, compare only what you are comfortable sharing; a fictional example is always allowed.
4. As a team, prepare one general example of a marketing influence.
5. Share two examples, then connect to the SBTE’s Chapter 1 lesson.

**Facilitation**

- Starter: 2 minutes of quiet writing, keywords, or sketching while the SBTE takes attendance.
- Partner exchange: 3 minutes; students may use a fictional purchase or pass and work from notes.
- Team synthesis: 3 minutes; optional roles are reader, recorder, timekeeper, and reporter.
- Debrief: ask, **“What did the business do, or what did the customer notice, that influenced the choice?”**
- Teaching link: the SBTE decides whether to name customer needs, promotion, price, place, product, or another Chapter 1 concept.

**Say this**

> There is no single correct answer, and you do not have to share a personal purchase. Start with a note or fictional example, compare ideas with a partner, and help your team prepare one general example.

### Option B — Marketing/business: “One industry or app: good, service, idea”

**Board-ready agenda**

1. Choose an industry or app your team knows.
2. Add one example of a **good**, one **service**, and one **idea**.
3. Write one reason for each classification; if unsure, mark it as a question.
4. Choose one example to explain to the class.
5. Connect the examples to the Chapter 1 distinction between business, product, marketing, good, service, and idea.

**Facilitation**

- Give teams 5–8 minutes; show the countdown and a one-minute warning.
- Use the editable table on slide 5 of the deck, or draw three columns on the board.
- Optional roles: reader, recorder, timekeeper, reporter; students may contribute by writing or pointing.
- Ask: **“What makes this a thing, a performance, or a thought?”** Do not correct immediately; let the team explain and then check against the book.
- If time allows, ask one bridge question: **“How might marketing help this industry or app reach customers?”**

**Say this**

> Your job is to classify examples and explain your reasoning. More than one answer may be defensible if your team can support it; we will check the definitions together.

### Option C — Principles and Tools / Office track: “Build a reliable workflow”

**Board-ready agenda**

1. Put these four steps in the order that helps a student complete unfamiliar work: **read the directions → try the step → ask a nearby peer → ask the teacher/check the result**.
2. Quietly choose your order and write one reason.
3. Compare with a partner.
4. As a team, agree on one workflow and one behavior that helps everyone participate.
5. Share the workflow; return to the SBTE’s existing Office/business agenda.

This uses the SBTE’s already-described support sequence as a team-building and problem-solving routine. It does not turn that sequence into a new rule or grade.

**Facilitation**

- Starter: 2 minutes of quiet ordering or writing while the SBTE takes attendance.
- Pair comparison: 3 minutes; each student can point to or read a step.
- Team agreement: 3–4 minutes; one recorder and one reporter are enough.
- Debrief: ask, **“What should someone try before they get stuck, and how can a team make asking for help easier?”**
- Transition: the SBTE decides whether to connect this to the textbook, guided notes, or existing Office work.

**Say this**

> There are several reasonable orders. First make your own choice, then compare it with a partner. Your team’s job is to explain one workflow, not to find a perfect answer.

### One team-building activity to offer either class: “Classify, explain, improve”

Use this only if the SBTE wants a separate team-building activity rather than one of the agenda starters.

Write one short challenge on the board: **“Choose one industry or app. Your team has five minutes to classify one good, one service, and one idea, explain one choice, and improve it after checking the definition.”** Give each team four optional roles: **reader, recorder, timekeeper, reporter**. Students may choose a role, swap roles, or contribute in writing. At the end, each team shares one classification and one behavior that helped the team work.

The activity is intentionally short, structured, and low-stakes. It practices listening, turn-taking, asking for clarification, explaining evidence, checking, and revising—the same habits that strengthen both CS thinking and business-course work. It is not a student-support plan and should not be framed as being “for ADHD students.”

## Use the deck without taking over the class

The revised [Chapter 1 + Microsoft 365 deck](2026-09-10-M365-Workflow-Team-Building.pptx) is a teaching tool, not a new curriculum. A simple sequence is:

1. **Slide 3:** the Chapter Review purchase question; use it as a short starter while attendance happens.
2. **Slide 5:** the editable goods/services/ideas table; use it for the industry/app team classification.
3. **Slides 7–9:** use only if the SBTE wants to extend the Chapter 1 discussion into marketing concept, the 4 Ps, or marketing functions.
4. **Slide 10:** model how a clear PowerPoint chart is built; students do not need to create a deck unless the SBTE assigns that work.
5. **Slide 11:** preview Friday's Microsoft 365 workflow—class folder, archive, meaningful file name, download, Teams upload. Do not launch that workflow early without the SBTE's direction.

The CS strategy is deliberately embedded: students decompose the prompt, classify examples, state a reason, check the book, and revise the chart. This aids the business lesson; it does not compete with it.

## Roles: choose one, not all four

1. **Introduce:** read the directions once, then let the SBTE continue.
2. **Time:** display a two-minute warning and a one-minute warning.
3. **Circulate:** ask, “What reason have you got so far?” and “Who has not had a chance to contribute?” Do not take over keyboards or require personal disclosure.
4. **Debrief:** collect two different reasons and hand the transition back to the SBTE.

Use the SBTE's existing proximity, private-check-in, nurse, attendance, and discipline routines. Do not diagnose, independently approve release, change attendance, or promise a grade.
