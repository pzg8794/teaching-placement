## Your job tomorrow in one sentence

Arrive with **two short agenda choices and one team-building activity**, check the morning plan with your SBTE, then take only one manageable role: introduce, time, circulate, or debrief.

These are prepared supports built from the September 9 direction. They are not new school requirements, grades, accommodations, or permission to replace the SBTE's plan.

- [ ] Bring this guide, a charged laptop/charger, notebook, and pen.
- [ ] Have the two agenda cards below ready to show or copy to the board.
- [ ] Ask which class is first, whether the block is still shortened, and which existing activity/material the SBTE wants used.
- [ ] Do not repeat the partner-interview deck unless the SBTE specifically wants a catch-up group to use it.

## Two ready-to-use, low-stakes agenda cards

Use only after the SBTE chooses one. Keep both ungraded unless the SBTE explicitly changes that.

### Option A — Principles and Tools / Office track: “Speed or accuracy?”

**Board-ready agenda**

1. Quick choice: when solving a problem, which matters more first—speed or accuracy?
2. Explain your choice to a partner.
3. As a team, write one behavior that helps everyone participate.
4. Share one idea; listen for a different reason.
5. Return to the SBTE's existing class expectations and today's plan.

**Facilitation**

- Starter: 3 minutes; students may write keywords instead of speaking first.
- Partner exchange: 2 minutes each way, or a shorter round if the block is tight.
- Team behavior: 4–6 minutes; one recorder or shared note is enough.
- Debrief: ask, “What helped the group hear more than one idea?”
- Transition: the SBTE connects the discussion to the existing course routine. Do not turn it into an Office assignment or a technology demonstration.

**Say this**

> There is no single correct choice. Give one reason, listen for another reason, and help your group make space for both.

### Option B — Intro to Marketing: “Same product, different choice”

**Board-ready agenda**

1. Two fictional cafés sell the same drink for the same price. Why might a customer choose one over the other?
2. List three possible reasons.
3. Rank the reason your group thinks matters most.
4. Be ready to explain the ranking in one sentence.
5. Connect the discussion to the SBTE's existing marketing agenda.

**Possible reasons if the room needs a prompt:** convenience, service, trust, design, or the overall experience. These are examples, not an answer key.

**Facilitation**

- Starter/write: 2–3 minutes.
- Pair or team list: 4–5 minutes.
- Rank and explain: 4 minutes.
- Whole-class share: 5 minutes, stopping while participation is still useful.
- Transition: the SBTE decides whether to continue into existing notes/reading or close the starter.

**Say this**

> Marketing is not only what a business sells. It also involves how people experience a choice. Give evidence for your reason, and remember that different customers can choose differently.

## Roles: choose one, not all four

1. **Introduce:** read the directions once, then let the SBTE continue.
2. **Time:** display a two-minute warning and a one-minute warning.
3. **Circulate:** ask, “What reason have you got so far?” and “Who has not had a chance to contribute?” Do not take over keyboards or require personal disclosure.
4. **Debrief:** collect two different reasons and hand the transition back to the SBTE.

Use the SBTE's existing proximity, private-check-in, nurse, attendance, and discipline routines. Do not diagnose, independently approve release, change attendance, or promise a grade.
