## Your job tomorrow in one sentence

Arrive with **two short agenda choices and one team-building activity**, check the morning plan with your SBTE, then take only one manageable role: introduce, time, circulate, or debrief.

These are prepared supports built from the September 9 direction. They are not new school requirements, grades, accommodations, or permission to replace the SBTE's plan.

## The clear teaching offer to make

Offer your SBTE this specific support:

> Yesterday you asked for two agendas, a short thought-provoking opening while you take attendance, and another team-building activity before Friday's technology work. I prepared a visible five-to-ten-minute routine for each class: students see the steps, get quiet think time, discuss with a partner or team, and finish with one share-out. I can put the agenda on the board, keep time, circulate, and hand the class back to you. Which version would help you most today?

This is the teaching contribution—not simply “I have an activity.” It gives students a predictable entry, reduces the amount they must hold in working memory, offers a way to begin privately before speaking, uses a visible time boundary, and moves from individual thinking to partner/team talk before whole-class sharing. These are universal classroom supports for attention and participation; they do not label students, diagnose ADHD, or create an accommodation without the SBTE/school process.

### What you are offering to do

1. Write or display the four-step agenda before students settle: **read the prompt → think/write → talk with a partner/team → share one idea**.
2. Give one direction at a time and point to the current step.
3. Provide a visible two- or three-minute timer and a one-minute warning.
4. Let students write, sketch, or quietly rehearse before speaking; do not require personal disclosure or cold-call a student.
5. Circulate with one neutral prompt: **“What is your group’s idea so far?”**
6. Collect only two or three different ideas, then hand the connection to the SBTE’s existing lesson.

Choose one role for yourself—board agenda, timer, circulation, or debrief. The SBTE remains responsible for attendance, behavior decisions, grading, and the lesson transition.

## Two ready-to-use, low-stakes agenda cards

Use only after the SBTE chooses one. Keep both ungraded unless the SBTE explicitly changes that.

### Option A — Marketing: “Why choose one business?”

**Board-ready agenda**

1. Two fictional cafés sell the same drink for the same price.
2. Quietly write or sketch: **Why might a customer choose one over the other?**
3. With a partner, list three possible reasons.
4. As a team, choose one reason and prepare one sentence.
5. Share two different reasons; connect back to the SBTE’s marketing lesson.

**Facilitation**

- Starter: 2 minutes of quiet writing, keywords, or sketching while the SBTE takes attendance.
- Partner list: 3 minutes; students may read from their notes rather than improvise.
- Team choice: 3 minutes; optional roles are reader, recorder, timekeeper, and reporter.
- Debrief: ask, **“How can different customers make different choices?”**
- Teaching link: name the connection only if the SBTE wants it—customer experience, convenience, service, trust, or design.

**Say this**

> There is no single correct answer. Start by writing one reason, then compare ideas with a partner. Your team only needs to agree on one reason to share.

### Option B — Principles and Tools / Office track: “Build a reliable workflow”

**Board-ready agenda**

1. Put these four steps in the order that helps a student complete unfamiliar work: **read the directions → try the step → ask a nearby peer → ask the teacher/check the result**.
2. Quietly choose your order and write one reason.
3. Compare with a partner.
4. As a team, agree on one workflow and one behavior that helps everyone participate.
5. Share the workflow; return to the SBTE’s existing Office/business agenda.

This uses the SBTE’s already-described support sequence as a team-building and problem-solving routine. It does not turn that sequence into a new rule or grade.

**Facilitation**

- Starter: 2 minutes of quiet ordering or writing while the SBTE takes attendance.
- Pair comparison: 3 minutes; each student can point to or read a step.
- Team agreement: 3–4 minutes; one recorder and one reporter are enough.
- Debrief: ask, **“What should someone try before they get stuck, and how can a team make asking for help easier?”**
- Transition: the SBTE decides whether to connect this to the textbook, guided notes, or existing Office work.

**Say this**

> There are several reasonable orders. First make your own choice, then compare it with a partner. Your team’s job is to explain one workflow, not to find a perfect answer.

### One team-building activity to offer either class: “One goal, four roles”

Use this only if the SBTE wants a separate team-building activity rather than one of the agenda starters.

Write one short challenge on the board: **“Your team has five minutes to agree on the best first step for a new student who is unsure how to begin today’s work.”** Give each team four optional roles: **reader, recorder, timekeeper, reporter**. Students may choose a role, swap roles, or contribute in writing. At the end, each team shares one first step and one behavior that helped the team work.

The activity is intentionally short, structured, and low-stakes. It practices listening, turn-taking, asking for clarification, and reaching agreement—the same participation habits the SBTE needs while building routines. It is not a student-support plan and should not be framed as being “for ADHD students.”

## Roles: choose one, not all four

1. **Introduce:** read the directions once, then let the SBTE continue.
2. **Time:** display a two-minute warning and a one-minute warning.
3. **Circulate:** ask, “What reason have you got so far?” and “Who has not had a chance to contribute?” Do not take over keyboards or require personal disclosure.
4. **Debrief:** collect two different reasons and hand the transition back to the SBTE.

Use the SBTE's existing proximity, private-check-in, nurse, attendance, and discipline routines. Do not diagnose, independently approve release, change attendance, or promise a grade.
