## School scale and its application

The September 24 SBTE explanation supplies additional classroom interpretation: proper terminology and meaningful connections matter, and 3 remains the expected level while 4 goes further. The same passage describes zero for a blank on a different review/test. That rule and the September 25 quiz bonus discussion are not silently imported into this presentation; the photographed IE definition and this assignment's reporting process still need reconciliation with the SBTE.

The school photograph describes:

- **4:** beyond explicitly taught curriculum or deeper complexity; greater independence than expected; independent transfer to unique situations.
- **3:** consistent, independent mastery of grade-level work; successful work with the expected guidance; transfer to new problems or next steps with guidance.
- **2:** inconsistent mastery; more guidance/scaffolding than expected; transfer appears only with prompting.
- **1:** an attempt without mastery yet; significant guidance to attend to the task; transfer not demonstrated yet despite support.
- **IE:** no task available to score.

The rubric translates these broad anchors into evidence for this specific assignment. It does not reproduce the photograph as a checklist of mandatory conditions. Level 4 cells offer examples of depth or transfer; they do not demand all routes, additional slides, or unassigned research. Supports that provide access are distinguished from an adult supplying the reasoning. The latter is documented; the former is not an automatic score ceiling.

**IE recommendation:** retain the explanation in teacher guidance, outside the four achievement columns. Confirm this assignment's reporting practice with the SBTE. Do not convert IE to zero, use it as a fifth achievement level, or assume that an incomplete but scorable attempt is IE.

## Recommendations to choose from

1. **Use separate content outcomes if school reporting permits.** Give communication feedback separately. This keeps the three assigned content targets visible. If a separately scored communication outcome is wanted, the fourth row is ready.
2. **Publish the selected scoring method before students begin.** The equal-average example is mathematical guidance, not a percentage rubric. A row total out of 16 is not proposed as the assignment's grade.
3. **Give students the guide, the selected rubric, and optional planning support.** Keep teacher grading choices and source commentary in the teacher copy.
4. **Offer a common short transfer prompt if evaluating 4 through transfer.** Let all students see this opportunity; do not require surprise questioning or a longer presentation. Give access supports and processing time consistently.
5. **Calibrate before live scoring.** Independently score two sample explanations, compare the cited evidence, and resolve disagreements about gaps, supports, and deeper reasoning. This is a proposed reliability check, not a claim that two-rater reliability has already been tested.

## Audience evidence and scaffolding decisions

**What can reasonably be said:** the recorded classes include students who can generate relevant familiar contexts, identify concrete customer advantages, and participate in short supported explanations. These are promising foundations for the assignment. Evidence of complete independent links among a function, customer needs/wants, company goals, and profit is uneven in the sampled exchanges, because teachers sometimes supply the connecting reasoning.

**What cannot be said:** these transcripts do not establish individual ability profiles, disability-related needs, motivation, all-student proficiency, or a common readiness level across both Marketing sections. Speaker attribution and transcription are imperfect. Reported quiz results include bonuses, so they cannot be treated as a clean measure of independent conceptual mastery. Word-processing classes are not a proxy for Marketing ability.

| Observed evidence | Recommendation grounded in UofR | Check that decides whether to keep support |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant examples emerge before full conceptual explanations. S6. | Begin with student-generated context, then ask for the business action and why it fits. Use the organizer as a support, not a compulsory extra assignment. W1, W3, W5. | Can the student explain the action without the adult supplying the causal link? |
| Links to customer experience/profit sometimes emerge with adult questions and elaboration. S6. | Offer one targeted prompt or frame, then ask for a fresh explanation. W1, W6. | Can the student connect customers and the business in their own explanation after the prompt is removed? |
| Vocabulary categories are blurred in some teacher-led exchanges. S7. | Contrast function, marketing mix, target market, and marketing concept with the actual textbook. Treat this partly as an instructional clarity problem, not only a learner deficit. W1, W4. | Can the student name the category and justify its use in a new action? |
| Repeated volunteers dominate part of the discussion. S8. | Think/write first, then sample across the room; allow a rehearsed response. W1, W2. | Has evidence been collected from students beyond the volunteers? Quietness is not an ability score. |
| A learner reports a submission-step mismatch. S9. | Add a brief file/destination/submission-status check separate from the content score. W4, W5. | Is the intended file actually in the assigned submission, and is its status confirmed? |

The support sequence is intentionally responsive: common clear targets and access options for everyone; targeted prompts when evidence shows a gap; extension and greater independence when the explanation is already secure. It is not a fixed high/medium/low grouping system. Familiar student contexts are retained, but complete answers are not handed out before students try.
