# Catch-Up and Co-Teaching Options — September 21–25, 2026

## Visible catch-up screen

Use one stable three-column screen at entry:

| WHERE WE ARE | WHAT YOU NEED | WHAT TO DO NEXT |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1: `[page/heading]` | Textbook, Chapter 1 notes, Terms to Know, Chapter #1 Review | 1. Open the page. 2. Check the last completed heading. 3. Complete the next marked item. 4. Ask after you have tried the source cue. |

Update the page/heading separately for Blocks 1, 5, and 7. Block 7's stopping point is unconfirmed. Never use a generic “we are on Chapter 1” message.

## 3–5 minute catch-up path

1. **Locate (30 sec):** scan the posted block-specific page, slide, and assignment.
2. **Check (60 sec):** compare the student’s last completed page/slide to the posted stop.
3. **Do next (2 min):** read the missing paragraph or complete one missing vocabulary item using the blue textbook term.
4. **Verify (30 sec):** show the highlighted sentence, note, or completed term to Piter/SBTE.
5. **Join (immediately):** enter the current task at the visible “join here” marker.

This is not a replacement for completing missed work; it is a re-entry ramp.

## By need

- **Absent student:** open the block’s recorded stop; read the named page range; complete the matching notes; then use the assignment list. No need to ask “What did I miss?” before checking the screen.
- **Missing vocabulary:** open Terms to Know and the textbook’s blue terms. Complete the next term as academic definition + student-friendly example/visual. Do not copy an unrelated web definition.
- **Behind in reading:** use the exact heading and paragraph cue. Read one chunk, write a five-to-seven-word gist, then rejoin.
- **Needs teacher help:** arrive with page open and point to the exact sentence/step causing difficulty. Teacher gives a cue or example, not a full repeat of yesterday’s class.
- **Finishes early:** improve one Review answer by adding a Chapter 1 term, an explanation, and a fictional example; or create a new scenario and label its 4 Ps/marketing function.

## Monday and Tuesday priority

- Monday: the revised plan reserves nine minutes for Terms to Know completion/conferencing; use a four-minute support group within that block.
- Tuesday: Terms is due at 9:00 AM; check the clock before describing work as late. Verify the assigned scope against content actually taught. Missing work uses the catch-up route without consuming the whole-class opener.
- Wednesday: Chapter #1 Review is due at 9:00 AM. Use one short SBTE station for missing/weak responses, then close it and return both teachers to the main lesson. Do not imply all review content was taught before this deadline.

## Entry alternatives, ranked for low preparation and low overrun risk

All replace the day's opener, not add to it. Use the same source concept and academic check; allow quiet written, pointing, or spoken responses. Finish by naming the exact page and heading.

| Rank / routine | Prep | Time | Participation | Reading connection | Access value | Overrun risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Where are we? | Update three fields | 2–3 min | Everyone opens/checks one item | Exact section stop | Predictable re-entry and planning | Very low |
| 2. A/B misconception check | One claim and two choices | 2 min | All choose, then one reason | Quote/paraphrase the day's concept | Low writing load; quiet think time | Low |
| 3. One-word retrieval | One previous academic term cue | 2 min | All write/point to word bank | Prior paragraph → next heading | Retrieval plus optional visual cue | Low |
| 4. Visual classification | Reuse three labeled pictures | 3 min | All classify | p. 4 G/S/I or pp. 5–7 activities | Image + label; no color-only code | Low |
| 5. Predict then verify | One fictional scenario | 3 min | All predict, then read evidence | pp. 10–12 concept or 4 Ps | Low-stakes entry before vocabulary | Low–medium |
| 6. Mini-case comparison | Two brief options | 3–4 min | All choose with one reason | pp. 13–15 functions | Familiar context; academic explanation retained | Medium; no open share-out |

Example misconception: “Place means only the building where a shop sits.” Agree/disagree, then use p. 12 to correct it. Hard stop for every routine: “Keep that idea. Now find the evidence under [heading] on page [number].”

## Small, repeatable co-teaching moves

| Piter's role | SBTE role | duration | purpose | handoff cue |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Lead the 3-minute opener | Tally A/B/C or fingers and note misconceptions | 3 min | Make participation visible without extending discussion | “Pat, what pattern did you see?” |
| Teach one UDL alternative slide | Restate the same concept with the SBTE course example | 2 min | Team-teach without duplicating a lecture | “Same concept—show them your example.” |
| Guide the required reading | Quietly support students entering at the catch-up marker | 5–6 min | Preserve reading while enabling re-entry | “At the next heading, everyone rejoins.” |
| Facilitate a scenario check | Gather class-level evidence on a clipboard/tally | 3 min | Separate teaching from observation | “What did the responses tell us?” |
| Support Terms/Review station | Continue core reading/application with the main group | 5 min | Address missing work without stopping class | “Station closes when the timer reaches zero.” |
| Explain one example | Offer a second representation or non-example | 90 sec | Clarify a misconception quickly | “Show us the non-example.” |
| Run closure/exit check | Confirm exact page/slide stop in the shared teacher record | 2 min | Prevent next-day guessing | “Record: slide __, page __, heading __.” |

## Guardrails

- Keep each move under six minutes.
- A support station has a visible end time and a clear rejoin point.
- Both teachers use the same academic terms and same required reading.
- Do not lower the required response for students at the station; support access, chunking, and entry.
- Record only class-level observations in the comparison log; student-specific concerns remain in private school systems.
