### Monday opener — “Which move comes first?” (3 minutes)

- **Student-facing task:** “A school snack shop wants more students to buy lunch. Which should it do first? A: copy the most popular shop; B: ask students what they need/want; C: make more of the same food. Show A, B, or C.”
- **Exact slide text:** `A school snack shop wants more students to buy lunch. What should it do FIRST? A Copy a competitor  B Ask students what they need/want  C Make more of the same food. Show A/B/C. Be ready with one reason.`
- **Expected response:** B. Marketing begins with understanding customer wants and needs; answers can note that research informs later choices.
- **Follow-ups:** “What could the shop ask?” “How could the answer affect the product?”
- **Hard stop:** “Hands down. We have our prediction; the chapter now gives us the academic model.”
- **Transition:** “Page 10 explains why marketing begins with customers, and slide 9 names the three parts of the marketing concept.”
- **Pages/terms:** pp. 10–11; purpose of marketing, marketing concept, customer needs, company goals, profits.
- **Time:** 3 minutes.

### Tuesday opener — “Odd one out: the 4 Ps” (3 minutes)

- **Student-facing task:** “A sneaker launch includes: A new color, B $65 price, C sold online and in stores, D employees receive training. Which is not one of the 4 Ps?”
- **Exact slide text:** `ODD ONE OUT — A New sneaker color  B $65  C Online + stores  D Employee training. Show A/B/C/D. Which choice is NOT one of the 4 Ps?`
- **Expected response:** D. A/B/C map to product/price/place; training may matter to the business but is not one of the 4 Ps.
- **Follow-ups:** “Which P is C?” “What would promotion look like?”
- **Hard stop:** “Lock it in. The four academic labels are product, price, place, and promotion.”
- **Transition:** “Use pages 11–12 to check each label and see what decisions marketers make inside each P.”
- **Pages/terms:** pp. 11–12; marketing mix, product, price, place, promotion.
- **Time:** 3 minutes.

### Wednesday opener — “Which function is working?” (3 minutes)

- **Student-facing task:** “A company surveys students before designing a backpack. Is that A promotion, B market research, or C selling?”
- **Exact slide text:** `A company surveys students BEFORE designing a backpack. Which marketing function is working? A Promotion  B Marketing Information Management / market research  C Selling. Show A/B/C.`
- **Expected response:** B.
- **Follow-ups:** “What information should it gather?” “Which later decision could this evidence change?”
- **Hard stop:** “We have the function. Keep the example; now we build the complete seven-function map.”
- **Transition:** “Page 13 names all seven functions and explains how market research connects customer evidence to product decisions.”
- **Pages/terms:** pp. 13–15; marketing functions, marketing information management, market research, target market.
- **Time:** 3 minutes.

### Thursday opener — “What should happen first?” (3 minutes)

- **Student-facing task:** “A company wants to launch a reusable water bottle. Rank the first two moves: A choose an ad, B learn what students want, C set a price, D choose stores.”
- **Exact slide text:** `REUSABLE BOTTLE LAUNCH — Choose the FIRST move, then the SECOND: A Choose an ad  B Learn what students want  C Set a price  D Choose stores. Write two letters: __ then __.`
- **Expected response:** B first. Reasonable second choices depend on evidence; students must justify the relationship.
- **Follow-ups:** “What could the research reveal?” “Which 4 P could change because of it?”
- **Hard stop:** “Stop at two moves. Today we test whether our sequence uses customer evidence, not whether everyone chose the same second step.”
- **Transition:** “Pages 13–15 show how specialized functions turn customer evidence into coordinated marketing decisions.”
- **Pages/terms:** pp. 13–15; MIM/market research, planning, pricing, product/service management, promotion, selling, channel management.
- **Time:** 3 minutes.

### Friday opener — “Four-box retrieval” (3 minutes)

- **Student-facing task:** “Without notes, write one word/example in each box: product; one business activity; one P; one marketing function. If stuck, draw or use a fictional example.”
- **Exact slide text:** `3-MINUTE RETRIEVAL — No notes yet. Fill four boxes: 1 Product example  2 Business activity  3 One of the 4 Ps  4 One marketing function. Words, short phrases, or a quick sketch are all acceptable.`
- **Expected response:** Any accurate example/term in each category.
- **Follow-ups:** “Which box was easiest?” “Which box should you check before the assessment?”
- **Hard stop:** “Pens down. This is a readiness check, not a new lesson.”
- **Transition:** “Use what you retrieved to focus; the assessment/review asks you to use the same Chapter 1 vocabulary and relationships.”
- **Pages/terms:** pp. 4–15; cumulative Chapter 1.
- **Time:** 3 minutes.
