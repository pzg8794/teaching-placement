## 1) Placement Overview & Phases

### Phase 1 — Observation & Assistantship (150+ hours)
**Primary intent:** Learn the classroom context, students, routines, and curriculum; contribute meaningfully as an apprentice teacher; begin co-teaching and small-group instruction.

**Candidate responsibilities in Phase 1 (examples):**
- Observe instruction and document professional learning through structured notes/reflections.
- Support students 1:1 and in small groups.
- Assist with lesson preparation, materials, and formative checks.
- Co-teach lesson segments or mini-lessons as agreed.

### Phase 2 — Student Teaching (5 weeks / 25 instructional days)
**Primary intent:** Gradual release of responsibility toward leading instruction; demonstrate growth across the CPAST pedagogy and dispositions domains.

**Candidate responsibilities in Phase 2 (examples):**
- Plan and deliver full lessons/lesson sequences, including differentiation and inclusive supports.
- Implement formative and summative assessments; analyze results to adjust instruction.
- Manage classroom routines with support from the cooperating teacher.
- Collect evidence/artifacts for evaluation and reflection.

## 5) Planning, Instruction, and Deliverables

### Professional expectations (always-on)
- **Attendance & punctuality:** arrive on time for committed blocks; communicate delays immediately.
- **Deadlines:** meet agreed planning/reflection deadlines; if something becomes infeasible, communicate early and propose an alternative.
- **Confidentiality:** follow school and program policies; do not share identifying student information in notes/artifacts.
- **Professional communication:** respectful tone; concise written updates; follow-through on commitments.

### Weekly planning expectations
- **Weekly planning packet due:** Candidate provides a weekly planning packet to Derek **by Friday (end of day)** for the following week.
  - If a school holiday or major assignment disrupts Friday submission, candidate and cooperating teacher agree on an alternative deadline in advance.
- **Materials readiness:** Candidate comes prepared with required materials, tech, printouts, and backups.

**Weekly planning packet (minimum contents):**
- Learning targets/objectives (student-friendly wording where appropriate)
- Outline of lesson flow (opener → modeling → guided practice → independent practice → closure)
- Inclusion/differentiation plan (UDL supports, scaffolds, accommodations/modifications as relevant)
- Formative checks for understanding (what, when, and what adjustment happens if students struggle)
- Materials/tech list (links, handouts, device needs)
- Assessment artifact (exit ticket, rubric, quick quiz, reflection prompt, or performance task)

### Instructional responsibilities (gradual release)
- Phase 1: observation + assistance, then co-teaching segments and small-group instruction.
- Phase 2: candidate leads full lessons/units with increasing independence.

### Inclusive practice expectations
Across both phases, the candidate will intentionally plan for:
- differentiation and access (UDL-informed supports, scaffolds, alternatives),
- culturally responsive and disability-responsive practices,
- safe and respectful learning environment.

### Candidate learning goals (aligned to evaluation)
The candidate will work toward the following goals during Spring 2026 (refined at midpoint):
- **Goal 1 — Planning & alignment:** Produce weekly plans with clear learning targets aligned to relevant standards/curriculum and coherent learning tasks.
- **Goal 2 — Inclusive instruction:** Implement consistent differentiation supports (scaffolds, choice, multimodal representations) and reflect on student access/engagement.
- **Goal 3 — Assessment & adjustment:** Use at least one routine formative check per lesson block and document at least one instructional adjustment per week based on evidence.
- **Goal 4 — Digital tools in CS learning:** Integrate age-appropriate digital tools/resources to support coding/CS learning and student collaboration.
- **Goal 5 — Reflective practice:** Maintain a weekly reflection log that connects instruction to research/theory and identifies one improvement action for the next week.

## 6) Evidence & Evaluation Alignment (CPAST + Field Experience)

This placement uses the CPAST framework to evaluate pedagogy and dispositions. Evidence may include (non-exhaustive):
- cumulative lesson plans with standards/objectives, differentiation, and assessment plans,
- observation notes and post-lesson reflections,
- instructional materials/handouts and appropriate citations,
- formative assessment checks, feedback examples, and adjustments made from data,
- artifacts demonstrating use of digital tools and learning technologies,
- documentation of professional behaviors (punctuality, meeting deadlines, collaboration, receptiveness to feedback).

**Candidate commitment:** Maintain an organized portfolio of evidence throughout the semester, with de-identified student work where applicable and consistent adherence to school policies.

**Suggested evidence cadence (lightweight, sustainable):**
- 1–2 lesson plans/week saved as PDF
- 1 example/week of formative assessment + resulting adjustment note
- 1 reflection/week (what worked, what didn’t, what changes next)
- Periodic screenshots/photos of non-identifying classroom materials or student-facing instructions (no faces/names)

## Appendix A — Evaluation “Look-Fors” (quick mapping)

This appendix is provided to make expectations concrete and to support efficient evidence collection.

**Planning & instruction**
- Standards/objectives and learning targets are explicit and aligned to tasks.
- Materials/resources fit learner needs; differentiation is planned, not improvised.

**Formative assessment & feedback**
- Routine checks for understanding are used; instruction is adjusted based on evidence.
- Learners receive actionable feedback.

**Digital tools**
- Digital tools support learning goals (not “tech for tech’s sake”) and are accessible to learners.

**Professional characteristics (field experience)**
- Attendance/punctuality matches this letter’s commitments.
- Communication is professional and timely; commitments are followed through.
- Candidate is receptive to feedback and demonstrates growth.
